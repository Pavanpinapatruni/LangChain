Transformer models have revolutionized deep learning by replacing recurrent connections with self-attention mechanisms, allowing for parallel computation and capturing long-range dependencies. Based on their architecture and training, they are generally categorized into Encoder-only, Decoder-only, and Encoder-Decoder (Seq2Seq) models.

Here is an explanation of the primary transformer variants:
1. Encoder-Only Transformers (Autoencoding Models)
These models process the input sequence entirely at once to build a meaningful, bidirectional representation of the data. They are excellent for language understanding, classification, and token-level tasks.

BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers): Google's seminal model that reads text bidirectional, considering both left and right context, making it superior for tasks like sentiment analysis, question answering, and named entity recognition.
RoBERTa (Robustly Optimized BERT Pretraining Approach): An improved training approach for BERT that performs better on general language understanding tasks.
Other variants: ALBERT (lightweight BERT), DistilBERT (faster, smaller BERT).

3. Encoder-Decoder Transformers (Sequence-to-Sequence)
These retain the original transformer architecture, using an encoder to understand the input and a decoder to generate the output, mapping one sequence to another.

4. Specialized and Efficient Transformers
Vision Transformer (ViT): Adapts the transformer architecture for computer vision by breaking images into patches and treating them as sequences. It achieves competitive results on image classification.
Transformer-XL: Introduced to allow learning longer-range dependencies than the original transformer by introducing a segment-level recurrence mechanism.
